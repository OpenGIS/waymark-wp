## open-editor

Map Meta is edited on the Map's own screen. Open the Map from Waymark, under Maps, and the Editor loads with its route. The fields underneath describe the trip. The Collections and Shortcode boxes sit alongside, and everything is saved with the Map.

## description

Description is the short summary. It sits above the groups and appears on the Map Details page. Journal Entry belongs to the About group, for the longer account: the road, the overnight stop and the final push. Both are rich text fields, so they can hold paragraphs, lists and links. A short Description is often enough; the Journal Entry can carry the story.

## details

Date and Distance sit in Details. Date is a plain text field, so you can use the format your readers expect. Distance is labelled in kilometres. Both render as plain text on the public page. The group headings keep a long list of fields tidy, and groups are optional: without them, every field stacks in one list.

## settings-fields

Every field is defined in Waymark Settings, under Meta. Each row sets a Meta Title, a Type, a Group, and whether the field appears in the Shortcode. The Tip explains the field in the Editor, and Default sets an initial value. This site defines four fields: Description, Journal Entry, Date and Distance.

## settings-groups

Groups are defined on the same tab. Drag them to re-order, or remove them all to turn grouping off. Their titles become the headings in the Editor and on the public Map, so a long list of Meta stays readable.

## front-map

The Map Details page is the public view of a single Map. The title and featured image come first; the map follows, and the meta sits underneath.

## front-meta

The meta renders below the map. Description and Collection appear above the groups, then the About group with Journal Entry. Date and Distance wait in Details. The Collection links are managed separately, in the Collections box.
