## open-editor

Markers are placed on the Map in the Editor. This Map carries thirteen Markers across six Types: six Photo, two Beer, two Alert, and one each of Food, Camp and Information. The icon comes from the Type, so a Photo Marker shows a camera and a Beer Marker shows a glass. The icon tells you the kind of place.

## editor-popup

Click a Marker to open its popup. This is The Scarlet Ibis, a Beer Marker. The popup holds the Marker Type, the title, the image and the description: "Good food and drink, especially after a long ride!" In the Editor, the popup is a form: the same fields, plus the coordinates and the Marker controls.

## settings-list

Marker Types live in Waymark Settings, under Markers. This site defines six: Beer, Food, Photo, Alert, Information and Camp. A Type is the shared style for every Marker that uses it. One definition covers every Marker of that Type.

## settings-row

Each row sets the Marker Label, the Shape, the Size and the Background, which is the Marker colour. Icon Type chooses a Font Icon, Text or HTML for custom content, including an image. Name holds the icon class, ion-camera for Photo, and Colour sets the icon colour.

## front-map

The public Map is the same Map, seen by a visitor. The title and featured image come first, and the Map sits below.

## front-markers

The same thirteen Markers sit at the public zoom. The icons and colours come from the same Types, so the six Photo Markers still read as photos, and Beer, Food, Alert, Camp and Information keep their own symbols.

## front-popup

Open The Scarlet Ibis here and the popup is the finished card: the title, the Type, the photo and the description. There are no fields and no controls. The Editor popup is the form; the public popup is what a visitor reads.

## front-popup-hold

A Type keeps iconography consistent. One definition covers every Marker of that Type, so you do not style each Marker on its own.
